**By the end of this act you can:** make parts of the agent's behaviour non-negotiable, and
scope what it's allowed to touch — so a powerful, probabilistic tool stays one you can trust.

Act 1 gave the agent the right *knowledge*. This act answers the other half — *what is the
agent allowed to do?* — by wrapping deterministic guardrails around its probabilistic output.

- **Hooks** — fire automatically at points in the agent's lifecycle; the one place the agent
  doesn't get an opinion. *(hands-on)*
- **Custom agents + MCP** — scope which tools and files an agent can reach (least privilege),
  and wire in fresh external knowledge. *(demo, via the "adding CDK the right way" vignette)*

## Control is what makes the partnership safe

<Aside type="tip" title="The theme">
The fear with agents is "it'll go off and do the wrong thing." Guardrails are the answer:
when you can set hard boundaries it can't cross, the agent becomes a partner you trust
rather than one you babysit. That's *control*, not fear.
</Aside>
